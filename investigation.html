Tulunad: The Jaffna of South India

There are lands conquered by armies. There are lands erased by maps. And there are lands slowly weakened by paperwork, resource extraction, and administrative silence. Tulunad belongs to the third category. The "Biological Frontier" is marked by a century of what activists term "Biological and Institutional Genocide." Between 1980 and 2001, state-sanctioned chemical attrition via Endosulfan claimed an estimated 30,000 souls in the Tuluva heartland. This was not merely an agricultural accident but a systemic failure where thousands were denied essential medical facilities because they spoke only Tulu and were met with the indifferent silence of a monolingual bureaucracy

Chapter I — The Biological Frontier

For decades, Tulu speakers have argued that language exclusion affects public access, documentation, and cultural continuity. When language is absent from institutions, citizens may experience exclusion not through force, but through bureaucracy.

Kempagidar Files Entry 402: The “Mindset of Negligency” describes a pattern where communities begin to internalize institutional neglect as normal.
This is a battleground of conflicting data. While the government’s Detailed Project Report (DPR) insists on a divertible yield of 24 TMC, independent scientific assessments, such as IISc's Technical Report 91, estimate the actual runoff to be a meager 9.55 TMC.[1, 2] For the coastal fishing community, this is an existential crisis. Traditional elders calculate their seasonal catch by the volume of "palke"—the nutrient-rich organic deposits brought by the river. A 30% reduction in freshwater flow would destroy this delicate salinity balance, likely triggering a mass migration of fish species and turning independent Tuluva fishers into mere laborers on deep-sea trawlers
