Tulunad: The Jaffna of South India

There are lands conquered by armies. There are lands erased by maps. And there are lands slowly weakened by paperwork, resource extraction, and administrative silence. Tulunad belongs to the third category.

Chapter I — The Biological Frontier

The "Biological Frontier" is marked by a century of what activists term "Institutional Attrition." The systemic failure where thousands were denied essential medical facilities because they spoke only Tulu is the bedrock of the current resistance. When language is absent from institutions, citizens experience exclusion not through force, but through bureaucracy.

Kempagidar Files Entry 402: The “Mindset of Negligency” describes a pattern where communities begin to internalize institutional neglect as normal.
TGIK Dispatch: Netravathi

Chapter II — The Netravati Frontier

The Ettinahole diversion is not merely an engineering issue. While the government insists on a divertible yield of 24 TMC, independent assessments estimate the actual runoff to be a meager 9.55 TMC. For the coastal fishing community, this is an existential crisis.

Chapter III — The Jaffna Parallel

Regional seers and activists describe the coastal districts as a "laboratory of experiments" for harmful state projects. Despite being high revenue generators, the region feels treated as a resource colony. The comparison to Jaffna is a reflection on how communities interpret administrative exclusion and historical memory.

Chapter IV — The 60-Day Blackout

The Action Taken Report (ATR) regarding the blackening of the Gurupura river remains unanswered. Institutional response has stalled, leading the National Green Tribunal (NGT) to demand a fresh report for the critical hearing scheduled for May 15, 2026.

Chapter V — Operation Mandamus

The "Kempagidar Files" expose a systematic "institutional capture" designed to turn Tulu into a kitchen dialect. The Tuluva Guardian dossier seeks to preserve both evidence and memory against the engineered collapse of regional sovereignty.
