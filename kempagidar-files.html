The Kempagidar Files

The Economic Assassination of Tuluva Sovereignty

I. The Man in the Red Shirt

In the 1920s, a figure known as "Kempagidar" (The Guy in the Red Shirt) became the nightmare of the regional administrative elite. S.U. Paniyadi was a Baroda-trained intellectual who mastered the "Science of Sovereignty" in the Baroda Central Library. He wore red to signal total defiance against the "Shudra Bhasha" stigma—a label used by Mysore elites to dismiss Tulu as a primitive dialect. Using his famous Voltairean wit, he mocked the "Kannadigized" elites who were ashamed of their mother tongue.

II. 1926: The Anthem of a Stolen Land

While Paniyadi built the armor, B. Monappa Thingalaya provided the soul. In 1926, Thingalaya composed the Land Anthem of Tulunadu. This anthem predates most state linguistic anthems in India, defining the historical borders of the Tuluva people. It was suppressed for decades because the elite feared a Tuluva identity that remembered its own geography and refused to be integrated into a subservient state project.

III. Sathi Kamale: The Masterpiece That Was Buried

Paniyadi proved Tulu was a high-literary language through his novel, Sathi Kamale. A work of Jnanpith-level quality, it was designed as an "Intellectual Shield" against those who claimed Tulu was "unlettered." After the takedown of Paniyadi, this masterpiece was purposefully kept out of the national spotlight, ensuring Tuluvas remained intellectually subservient.

IV. The Tuluva Mahasabha (1928)

Paniyadi founded the Mahasabha with radical demands: Constitutional status, reclamation of the Tulu-Tigalari script, and financial independence. He understood that a language without capital is a ghost. He established a Bank and the Manipal Press (Tulunadu) to give his people economic teeth.

V. The Great Betrayal

The tragedy was not just the enemy outside, but the betrayal within. As the elite engineered a bank run, Paniyadi's inner circle abandoned him to save their social status. During the Nehru Committee meetings, figures like Jinanath Hegde were present, yet the Tuluva voice was traded away. Icons like Kayyara Kinhanna Rai later realized the "Plot Shift" and the betrayal of the Paniyadi vision, but only after the institutions were lost.

VI. The 60-Year Blackout

After the engineered collapse, the press was "Kannadigized." For 60 years, not a single Tulu book—not even a child's story—was printed. This broke the linguistic chain between generations, turning Tuluvas into "Intellectual Orphans" in their own land.

"We are picking up the pen he was forced to drop, singing the anthem B. Monappa Thingalaya wrote 100 years ago, and reclaiming the press that was stolen from our children."

VII. The Return of the Guardian

Today’s Tulu activism follows the Paniyadi Path: Economic sovereignty, administrative defiance via the Tulu-Tigalari script, and the Voltairean spirit that refuses to accept the "Shudra" label. The investigation is over. The reclamation begins.
