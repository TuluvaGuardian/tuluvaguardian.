Dossier 2026-001: The Economic Assassination of S.U. Paniyadi

I. The Man in the Red Shirt

In the 1920s, Srinivasa Upadhyaya Paniyadi donned his signature red shirt to signal total defiance against the "Shudra Bhasha" stigma. Trained in Baroda, he knew that a culture without its own bank and press was a culture marked for death. He remains the architect of Tuluva intellectual resistance.

II. 1926: The Anthem of a Stolen Land

While Paniyadi built the intellect, B. Monappa Thingalaya provided the soul. In 1926, he composed the Land Anthem—a declaration of geographical sovereignty that the administrative machine has tried to erase for a century.

III. Sathi Kamale: The Masterpiece That Was Buried

Paniyadi’s novel Sathi Kamale proved Tulu's literary power. When his institutions were sabotaged, this work was buried to ensure Tuluvas remained "intellectual orphans." Reclaiming this book is a political necessity.

IV. The Tuluva Mahasabha & The Betrayal

Founded in 1928, the Tuluva Mahasabha demanded constitutional status. However, the movement faced internal sabotage. During the Nehru Committee meetings, Tuluva interests were traded away by leaders like Jinanath Hegde. It was a "Plot Shift" that Kayyara Kinhanna Rai only realized when the sovereign institutions were already lost.

V. The 60-Year Blackout

Following an engineered bank run, the Tulunadu Press was hijacked. For 60 years, not a single Tulu book reached our children. This breaking of the linguistic chain was a deliberate act of cultural capture.

"We are picking up the pen he was forced to drop, singing the anthem B. Monappa Thingalaya wrote 100 years ago, and reclaiming the press that was stolen from our children."
