The Kempagidar Files

Dossier 2026-001: The Economic Assassination of S.U. Paniyadi

I. The Man in the Red Shirt

In the 1920s, a figure known as "Kempagidar" (The Guy in the Red Shirt) became the living nightmare of the regional administrative elite. Srinivasa Upadhyaya Paniyadi used his Voltairean wit to strike back at those who mocked Tulu. Trained in Baroda, he knew that a culture without its own bank and press was a culture marked for death.

II. 1926: The Anthem of a Stolen Land

A century ago, B. Monappa Thingalaya gave Tulunadu its soul. He composed the Land Anthem, a declaration of coastal borders and pride that the administrative machine tried to erase for 100 years. It stands as the spiritual blueprint for the Tuluva Guardian movement today.

III. Sathi Kamale: The Masterpiece That Was Buried

Paniyadi’s novel Sathi Kamale proved Tulu was a high-literary language capable of profound philosophy. When his institutions were sabotaged, this book was buried to ensure Tuluvas remained "unlettered" in the eyes of the state. Reclaiming this book is reclaiming our intellectual rights.

IV. The Tuluva Mahasabha & The Great Betrayal

In 1928, Paniyadi founded the Tuluva Mahasabha. His demands were radical: Constitutional status and the Tulu-Tigalari script. However, he was betrayed by those close to him. During the Nehru Committee meetings, figures like Jinanath Hegde were present, yet Tuluva interests were traded away. Icons like Kayyara Kinhanna Rai later realized the "Plot Shift" and the loss of Paniyadi’s vision far too late.

V. The 60-Year Blackout

After the engineered bank run, Paniyadi’s Tulunadu Press was hijacked. For 60 years, not a single Tulu book reached our children. This institutional capture was the ultimate victory of those who sought to break the linguistic chain between generations.

"We are picking up the pen he was forced to drop, singing the anthem B. Monappa Thingalaya wrote 100 years ago, and reclaiming the press that was stolen from our children."
