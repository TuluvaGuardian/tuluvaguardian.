Dossier 2026-001: The Economic Assassination of S.U. Paniyadi

I. The Man in the Red Shirt

In the 1920s, Srinivasa Upadhyaya Paniyadi used his signature red shirt to signal total defiance against the "Shudra Bhasha" stigma. Trained in Baroda, he recognized that without a sovereign bank and press, Tuluva culture would be silenced by regional monopolies. He was the intellectual architect of coastal resistance.

II. 1926: The Anthem of a Stolen Land

While Paniyadi built the intellectual armor, B. Monappa Thingalaya provided the soul. In 1926, he composed the Land Anthem, a declaration of geographical pride that defined the historical borders of the Tuluva people—a memory the administrative machine has tried to erase for 100 years.

III. Sathi Kamale: The Buried Masterpiece

Paniyadi’s novel Sathi Kamale proved Tulu was a high-literary language. When his institutions were sabotaged, this work was buried to ensure the Tuluva people remained "intellectual orphans." Reclaiming this prose is an act of administrative defiance.

IV. The Tuluva Mahasabha & The Betrayal

Founded in 1928, the Tuluva Mahasabha demanded constitutional recognition. Yet, the movement was sabotaged from within. During the Nehru Committee meetings, figures like Jinanath Hegde were present, but the Tuluva interest was traded for political favors. Kayyara Kinhanna Rai and others realized the gravity of this "Plot Shift" only after the sovereign institutions were lost.

V. The 60-Year Blackout

Following an engineered bank run, the Tulunadu Press was hijacked. For 60 years, not a single Tulu book reached the hands of our children. This institutional capture ensured the breaking of the linguistic chain between generations.

"We are picking up the pen he was forced to drop, singing the anthem B. Monappa Thingalaya wrote 100 years ago, and reclaiming the press that was stolen from our children."
